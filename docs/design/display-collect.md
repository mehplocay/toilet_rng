# Display and income collection fix

2026-10-06 income-first update: [Economy v2](economy-v2.md) supersedes the historical rates, prices, offline cap and timing assertions below. Collection, protection, lease and reset contracts remain.

2026-10-06, `fix/display-collect`, baseline `b91a3bb82be41ef7507f88ef46b0f8b50caf3404`. No commit or push. [API research](../research/display-collect.md).

## Root causes and reproduction

1. Collection treated an item ID as a toggle: after finding any assigned slot, Display changed to Remove Display and sent `(nil, existingSlot)`. The server already reserved copies individually and allowed the protected first copy. SafeInventory correctly used `owned - max(first/protected copies, displayed copies)` for sales; weakening that protection would have fixed the wrong layer. Pedestal Place/Put Back prompts and explicit slot selection were absent in this worktree.
2. Pads were decorative, nontouchable anchors with prompts wired to the no-argument aggregate remote. There was no per-pad collection path. Very low rates, whole-coin collection, five-second refresh/input intervals and toast-only success made the feature appear ineffective. The jar already had correct ownership attributes, a ten-stud radius and RequiresLineOfSight=false; source tests do **not** establish an engine visibility defect. Its position is off the approach at local (-6, 0.15, 5), with a prompt anchor two studs above that datum.

`powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/check-audit.ps1 -DisplayCollectBaseline` loads the pinned original production source. Three focused cases produce **1 pass, 2 expected failures**: server copy/protection passes; the actual Collection callback and per-pad collection fail. Current source passes these and the full suite.

## Behavior and transaction contract

- Display adds one unreserved owned copy; Put Back removes one assignment. Protected first/rebirth copies remain displayable. Shared `Displayed`/`Displayable` helpers keep client/server counts consistent. Known-item, integer-slot, capacity, empty-slot and ownership validation remain authoritative. Collection has a Slots picker; pedestals use E/X Place or Put Back. Place selects the correct slot and clears stale rewards/search/filter state. Placing/removing does not change inventory counts.
- A green pad collects only its saved slot through F/Y/native touch prompt or owner-character contact. Ten physical positions preserve saved indexes on legacy pages. Locked slots have no active prompt/sensor. Earned income survives item removal. Each duplicate gets a separate model and nametag with its actual capped `$N/s` rate.
- `CollectIncome()` means central Collect All; `CollectIncome(integerSlot)` means that visible unlocked pad. Extra arguments, nil-as-an-argument, strings, instances/tables, NaN/infinity, off-page/locked slots and stale points are rejected. Server checks exact target distance (jar 10, pad prompt 6, contact 4.5 studs), owner, living character and lease. Pad proximity cannot authorize Collect All. Client ownership filtering is tested but is not authorization.
- Debit pending, credit Coins/Earned and snapshot before yielding. A second collection cannot run during that save. Success requires an acknowledged save and the same live profile. Busy manual input gives retry feedback. Failed/ambiguous saves preserve existing fail-closed behavior and never roll back a possibly committed transfer. Pad collection retains its fractions; Collect All pools fractions across slots, including hidden/removed assignments.
- Shared touch/prompt input budget: one token, refill 2/second. Internal durable-write budget: ten tokens, refill 0.2/second, allowing a ten-pad sweep but only 12 writes/minute sustained. Empty limb contacts use neither bucket. Existing per-Player cleanup removes buckets. Held saves cannot enqueue collect waiters; busy contacts require another step/prompt.
- Jar/HUD pending numbers refresh each second without rebuilding State/cards. Jar and individual pads pulse at >=1 whole coin using the client distance/streaming lifecycle. Success invokes CoinCollect, +N popup and five short-lived coin flights into the wallet. Existing audio settings and asset configuration apply; no IDs were invented.

## Balance and assumptions

Item rate is `2 * sell value` coins/minute before cash/caps; presentation uses per second. Tier slot caps are 5/7/11/18/34/67/141 coins/minute; player caps are ten times these. Base storage is 33,840/338,400 coins per slot/player, scaling with cash and Offline Tank. Offline elapsed cap remains 240 minutes, up to 480 upgraded. Persisted 6,000 units/coin, timestamps and old pending amounts remain; storage never shrinks.

- Ten reasonable retained items yield **22.01–31.74%** of expected active income across seven tiers with either no tracks or normal progression tracks through L4. Even ten Common Poop at Basic meet 20–35%. The established-showcase scenario uses the best copies from floored expected drops over 60 active minutes, not a free starting inventory.
- Absolute cap: **37.93%** worst case, including 100 retained jackpots, 0–15 rebirths and independent Cash/Speed levels 0–10. Active means service plus per-copy-rounded expected sales at 75% manual cooldown uptime, with the same cash/luck/speed state. This is an expected-rate comparison, not a promise against every random short-run outcome, idle player or slower auto-only policy. Extra luck improves the active comparator.
- Original 2,000-player **active-only** stage assertions remain unchanged: ±5% of 1.40/4.48/9.36/17.87/37.11/47.29 minutes. Toilet prices, service coins, sale values, upgrade prices and rebirth requirements are unchanged. The original 500-player rebirth cohort remains 71.06/62.29 minutes, retaining the 60–90-minute first-run and second-run-improvement assertions.
- Additional 200-player cohorts retain best-owned copies, sell only unreserved duplicates, accrue through the production ledger, and collect once/minute. Three and ten already-available slots are tested; no daily/offline starting wallet or slot-purchase costs are included. Retention initially costs sales; passive income intentionally speeds later purchases. These trajectories are reported separately from the original active-only targets and checked against a bounded progression envelope. Both display-enabled rebirth cohorts still assert 60–90 minutes and >8% second-run improvement. Exact medians are in the output.

- The requested 40% expected-active ceiling supersedes the old 20%-of-service-only cap and obsolete timing-shortening bound. Those old caps conflict with the requested passive rate. Original active progression target definitions are preserved, rather than silently changing their cohorts.
